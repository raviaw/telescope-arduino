🔭 Telescope Mount — ESP32 Port

Replacing the Arduino Mega 2560 + two encoder Nanos + HC-05 + RTC with a single ESP-WROOM-32

telescope-arduino / main_sketch

Design · 2026-07-01 · target board: DOIT ESP32 DevKit V1 (30-pin)

This document is the complete reference for porting the mount controller firmware to a classic ESP32. It covers the hardware inventory, the wiring and schematics, the pin map, and every firmware change — enough to build and flash the new controller without re-deriving anything.

Revision — elevated scope (major rework approved). Scope was raised from a minimal-change port to a proper rework (rewiring accepted). Where later sections differ, these win:
  • Modular firmware re-architecture — HAL + focused modules, no global soup (see §9), keeping the proven astronomy/calibration/backslash logic.
  • TB6600 rewired common-cathode — active-high STEP/DIR at 3.3 V.
  • Driver ENABLE wired per-axis (GPIO16/17) for idle auto-disable.
  • 2× MCP23017 (0x20 inputs, 0x21 outputs) for headroom.
  • NVS calibration persistence is now core scope.
  • Bluetooth telemetry fully implemented with clean JSON framing.

1 · Goal & rationale

Today the mount is driven by three microcontrollers: an Arduino Mega 2560 plus two Arduino Nanos that do nothing but read one quadrature encoder each and stream the position over serial. Add-on modules handle Bluetooth (HC-05) and timekeeping (DS3231 RTC). The goal is to collapse all of that into one ESP-WROOM-32.

Bluetooth Classic

The ESP32 speaks SPP/RFCOMM on-chip, so the GreatGrandeurs Android app connects exactly as it did to the HC-05 — no app changes.

PCNT hardware

8 hardware pulse counters read both encoders (and the menu knob) directly, deleting the two Nanos and the custom serial protocol.

64-bit doubles

The ESP32 does true double precision, fixing the AVR limitation the project README lists as future work. WiFi is a bonus (optional NTP).

5 · Encoder schematic (open-collector)

The YT3806 outputs are NPN open-collector: the transistor only pulls the line to 0 V; the high level is set by an external pull-up. That's what makes a 12 V-powered encoder safe for a 3.3 V MCU — pull the signal to 3.3 V, not to 12 V.

Hard rule: the pull-up resistors go to 3.3 V. Pulling OUT A / OUT B up to the 12 V encoder supply would put 12 V on an ESP32 input and destroy it.
Why no level shifter is needed: open-collector outputs don't drive a voltage — they only sink to ground. The pull-up alone defines the logic-high level, so a single resistor to 3.3 V does the whole job. The chosen pins (34/35/36/39) are input-only with no internal pull-ups, so the external resistor is required regardless.

Resolution: 1000 PPR × 4 (quadrature decode) = 4000 counts/rev, matching the old Encoder.read() behavior, so all downstream calibration/backlash math is unchanged.

6 · I²C bus topology

One bus, five to six devices, no address collisions. Two 4.7 kΩ pull-ups to 3.3 V for the whole bus.

7 · Power rails & grounding

Power tree (reuses existing supplies)

  • 24 V · 15 A Cheng Liang P360W24V → TB6600 VCC (motors).
  • 12 V · 20 A Durawell S-240-12 → encoder V+, and feeds the 5 V reg.
  • 5 V LM1117T-5.0 (existing) → ESP32 VIN.
  • 3.3 V AMS1117-3.3 (existing, from 5 V) → all I²C devices, I²C + encoder pull-ups, and the TB6600 opto common.

Grounding & levels

All grounds common: ESP32 GND, encoder Black/Shield, TB6600 GND, both PSU 0 V — else the open-collector encoder and STEP/DIR signals have no reference.

Power the I²C devices from 3.3 V so the bus idles at 3.3 V; at 5 V they would put 5 V on the ESP32 SDA/SCL and damage it.

TB6600 wiring (revised — common-cathode): tie all – pins (PUL-, DIR-, ENA-) to GND and drive the + pins from the ESP32 — active-high STEP/DIR at 3.3 V, no 5 V common, no DIR inversion. ENA is now wired per-axis (GPIO16/17) for auto-disable. At ≤7 kHz, 3.3 V opto drive is comfortable (74HCT125 buffer only as a fallback).
ESP32 ADC note: ADC2 pins are unusable while WiFi/Bluetooth is active. This design keeps all analog on the ADS1115, so the constraint never bites.

A software adapter scales the 16-bit reading into the 0–1023 range the existing translatePotValueToSpeed() expects, so that logic is untouched. The unused SPEED_POT stays dropped.

2× MCP23017 — digital I/O (revised)

ChipLines
0x20 inputskeypad UP/DOWN/LEFT/RIGHT/SELECT + action, knob-push, L-joy, R-joy, enable-pot
0x21 outputs4 status LEDs + laser + spare

Two chips (split inputs/outputs) give comfortable headroom instead of one maxed 16/16 chip. The breathing LED needs PWM, so it stays native on GPIO13.

All three offload chips (ADS1115, MCP23017, and — if the LCD were kept — a PCF8574 backpack) are stocked by Brazilian retailers (Eletrogate, MakerHero, Curto Circuito, Usinainfo, RoboCore/FilipeFlop, Mercado Livre); no import required.

10 · What stays identical

Astronomy math (calculateEverything, Ephemeris — now with true 64-bit doubles), the calibration flow, the backlash-compensation algorithm, the star catalog, and the entire Bluetooth JSON protocol. The Android app needs no changes.

11 · Optional / Phase 2

  • NVS calibration persistence — re-enable the stubbed EEPROM save/load using ESP32 Preferences so calibration survives power-off. Recommended, deferrable.
  • WiFi/NTP time and reviving telescope-server over TCP — enabled by the hardware, not built in this port.

12 · Open confirmations & risks

Open confirmations (do not block the architecture)

  1. TB6600 wiring — resolved: rewire common-cathode; ENA wired per-axis; keep DIP switches.
  2. OLED addresses — resolved: 2nd OLED set to 0x3D via its 0x78/0x7A jumper.
  3. Power rails — resolved: 24 V motors + 12 V encoders; LM1117-5.0 → 5 V (ESP32 VIN), AMS1117-3.3 → 3.3 V (I²C, pull-ups, opto common).

Risks

  • 2× MCP23017 (0x20 inputs / 0x21 outputs) — ample digital headroom.
  • BluetoothSerial + WiFi share the radio; if NTP is added, fetch time only at connect or verify coexistence.
  • Porting the LCD menu to OLED is the largest single code task; it is well-contained behind the existing printLcd* helpers.
